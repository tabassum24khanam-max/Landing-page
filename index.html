    01 How it works

    It reasons. It doesn't just react.

    No single model guesses. A market read, a case built against it, and a decision that has to survive its own scrutiny before anything is risked — with a mechanical layer guarding every position in between.

    01 · THE MATH

    Market read

    Reads price action, volume and structure across the board, forms a view, and has to state the strongest argument against its own conclusion before it counts for anything.

    • Approachview, then self-attack

    02 · THE NEWS

    Context read

    Separates what actually moves a market from what's just noise, and judges whether the market has already priced it in — because only the surprises matter.

    • Judgespriced in, or not

    03 · THE JUDGE

    The ruling

    Weighs both reads against a neutral risk picture, can overrule either one with a stated reason, and is cross-checked against itself before a position is ever opened.

    • Default stateflat

    Between decisions — mechanical, always on

    The risk layer

    Guards every open position continuously, with or without the AI.

    • Stops that move with the trade, never against it
    • Won't be shaken out by a two-second spike
    • Never stacks the same bet twice
    • Shrinks itself in markets going nowhere
    • Tightens the instant real news breaks
    • Flips a position when the thesis genuinely does

    Always watching

    between AI cycles

    A lightweight watcher stays on every open position and only interrupts the full reasoning early for something that actually matters — not for noise.

    Proven against a control

    not guessed at

    Two independent versions of the reasoning run side by side on separate books, scored against each other in real time before either one is trusted alone.

    Built to be checked, not believed

    The audience for a trading bot is skeptical, correctly. Four features exist purely to make the system checkable.

    Real equity vs. headline equity

    Most bots flatter their number by banking winners and quietly holding losers off-screen. BOBCAT shows both, always: the headline figure, and the true mark-to-market value including every open loss.
